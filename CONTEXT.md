# Domain Context

## Canonical Terms

- **Building**: An indivisible physical structure represented by one input point. A building carries the number of dwelling units it contains and is assigned wholly to one candidate EA.
- **Dwelling**: A census-relevant apartment, address, or other dwelling unit inside a building. Dwelling count is the primary candidate EA workload measure.
- **Candidate EA**: A proposed Enumeration Area generated by this tool for cartographer and census-authority review. It is not official until accepted through the governing review process.
- **Official EA**: An adopted Enumeration Area maintained by the census authority. This tool does not overwrite or silently replace it.
- **Administrative unit**: A polygon identified by the user-selected administrative ID field. Administrative units are binding containment constraints for candidate EAs.
- **Hard barrier**: A geographic feature that candidate EAs must not cross, such as a configured river or major road.
- **Soft barrier**: A feature that increases merge or travel effort but may be crossed when required to form a practical candidate.
- **Effort**: A transparent derived measure of enumeration difficulty. It keeps dwelling workload, building count, and distance components separate from the weighted total.
- **Lineage**: The trace from source buildings and configuration through candidate assignment, diagnostics, and later review decisions.
- **Review status**: The governance state of a candidate proposal, separate from its workload threshold status.

## Relationships

Each building belongs to exactly one administrative unit and exactly one candidate EA. A building may contain multiple dwellings, but its dwellings remain together because the building is the atomic assignment record. Candidate EAs group buildings and must not cross administrative units or configured hard barriers.

Threshold status describes whether dwelling workload is undersized, within range, oversized, or invalid. Review status describes whether a candidate is proposed or requires human review. These concepts must not be conflated.
